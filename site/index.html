Fly your rocket before launch day.

HPR Sim is a free, open-source flight simulator for hobby and high-power rockets. Give it your OpenRocket file: it flies the design, then tells you how far to trust each number.

Start with HPR Sim Take the tour

Checked against 60 real flights

On 60 logged flights of fliers' own designs, flown in each day's weather, HPR Sim's apogee came within 10% on 32. On average it read 7.8% high. It read low on 15, by at most 20%.

I write down every gap I know of, with its issue, and the command line warns you when your flight comes near one. Before you rely on a number, read how far to trust a simulation, or every check on the accuracy page.

Read the flight in your terminal

One command flies the design file and prints what you need on the pad: stability, apogee, rail exit speed and descent rate, in metric with feet beside.

$ hpr sim level-1.ork Level 1 guide rocket (level-1.ork) configuration 1 of 2: [H170M-10] a simulated flight, not a measurement

Every result says it is a simulation, and so does every file it exports.

static margin1.70 calibers off the rail apogee1065.1 m (3494 ft) above the site at 11.62 s rail exit speed26.3 m/s (86 ft/s) descent4.4 m/s (15 ft/s) at landing under `Parachute` […] warning: drag: issue #67: a cone-like nose's or shoulder's pressure drag reads high from Mach 0.8 […] This flight reaches Mach 0.84 at 1.7 s

Near a known gap in the physics, it names the issue and says which way the numbers lean.

What comes next

HPR Sim is the first part of FusionSpace HPR, one suite for hobby and high-power rockets, written in Rust. Every product's source and releases live in one repository, nrdptel/fusionspace-eridanus. Only HPR Sim is released so far.
